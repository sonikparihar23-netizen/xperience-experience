Dubai real estate after the DFM crash: what the data actually shows

The DFM real estate index fell about 30% in two weeks and sparked alarm. Ground-level transaction data for Q1 2026 shows a market that paused, not one that stopped.

Apr 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways
  • The DFM index tracks developer shares, not property prices
  • Q1 2026 residential sales rose 23.4% year on year
  • Most delayed deals were postponed, not cancelled

A sharp fall and a loud reaction

In the space of two weeks, the Dubai Financial Market's real estate index dropped roughly 30%, from around 16,800 points to 11,700. Social media and group chats filled with the same question: is Dubai real estate in trouble?

To answer it properly, you need to separate what the index measures from what is happening in actual property transactions.

What the DFM index measures, and what it does not

The index tracks the share prices of listed real estate companies. It reflects equity sentiment, and equity markets reprice within seconds of a geopolitical headline.

Physical property works differently. A sale involves viewings, negotiation, mortgage approval and Dubai Land Department registration over weeks or months. That time gap is the key dynamic any serious investor needs to understand.

What Q1 2026 transaction data shows

While the index was falling, the property market was posting strong numbers. These results were produced through the geopolitical noise, not despite it.

  • Residential salesAED 176.7 billion across nearly 48,000 transactions, up 23.4% year on year
  • Primary villa pricesMedian up 35.3% year on year to AED 4.1 million
  • Foreign investmentAED 148.4 billion from buyers of more than 150 nationalities
  • Off-plan shareAbout 70 to 71% of total transaction value

Postponed, not cancelled

There was an impact on the ground. Transaction volumes dipped about 25% in the first half of March as regional hostilities peaked, and some secondary pockets saw corrections of 15 to 20%, mostly among sellers wanting a fast exit.

But brokers across the market reported that most slowed deals were postponed rather than cancelled. Demand stayed in place and capital paused instead of leaving.

What this means for you

With a ceasefire in place, the conditions behind the sentiment shock are easing. Dubai's regulation, safe-haven status, rental yields and economic base have not changed.

If you are buying a home to live in, your core decision on location, liveability and value is the same. If you are investing, the secondary market may offer one of the more rational entry points of the past 18 months.

Xpert tip Look for sellers who priced for a fast exit during the dip. Compare their asking price with recent registered sales in the same building before you negotiate.
