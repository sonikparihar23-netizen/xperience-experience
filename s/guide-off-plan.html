Off-plan guide

Buying off-plan in Dubai, step by step.

How off-plan works, how payment plans and escrow protect you, and what happens from booking to handover.

Key takeaways
  • Off-plan means buying from the developer before the building is finished.
  • Payment plans spread the cost over construction, and some run past handover.
  • Your payments go into an escrow account and your purchase is registered on Oqood.
  • Foreigners, including non-residents, can own off-plan freehold outright.
Chapter 01

What off-plan means

An off-plan property is one you buy from the developer while it's still under construction. Buyers are drawn by prime locations, strong design and, in some projects, partnerships with well-known design brands.

Foreign buyers, including non-residents, can own off-plan property outright in Dubai's freehold areas.

Chapter 02

Why buyers choose off-plan

Buying during construction usually means a lower entry price than a finished home, with room for capital growth as the project takes shape. Completed projects in good locations often become sought-after homes and rentals.

The trade-off is time: you wait for handover before you can live in it or earn rent.

Xpert tip Look at the developer's past projects. Delivery record and build quality matter as much as the brochure.
Chapter 03

How payment plans work

Instead of paying in full, you pay in instalments tied to the construction timeline. Many developers also offer post-handover plans, so you can move in and keep paying the balance afterwards.

Plans vary by project, so compare the total cost and the timing of each payment, not just the deposit.

  • BookingReservation deposit to secure the unit
  • During constructionInstalments linked to build milestones
  • HandoverBalance due on completion
  • Post-handover plansSome developers spread payments after you move in
Chapter 04

How your money is protected

Dubai law requires developers to hold buyers' payments in a dedicated escrow account for each project, released as construction progresses. Your purchase is registered with the Dubai Land Department on the Oqood system, the off-plan equivalent of a title deed.

As with any purchase, the 4% DLD fee applies when your contract is registered.

  • RegistrationOqood, with the DLD
  • Your paymentsHeld in the project's escrow account
  • DLD fee4% of the purchase price
Xpert tip Always pay into the project's escrow account, never a personal or general company account.
Chapter 05

How to buy off-plan

The process is close to buying a ready home, with two extra stages: the initial booking and the handover. You reserve the unit, sign the sale and purchase agreement with the developer, register on Oqood and then pay to the agreed plan.

An off-plan consultant can compare projects, payment plans and developers so you choose with confidence.

Chapter 06

Mortgages for off-plan

Yes, you can get a mortgage on most off-plan properties in Dubai, but specific conditions apply and not every lender or project qualifies.

Speak to your consultant early so your financing fits the payment plan, especially the larger payment due at handover.

Chapter 07

Handover and after

When the building is complete, the developer invites you to inspect the unit, settle the balance and collect the keys. Once fully paid, your Oqood registration is replaced by a title deed.

Some off-plan property from approved developers can count towards a 10-year golden visa when it's worth AED 2 million or more.

Xpert tip Book a professional snagging inspection before you accept handover, so defects are fixed at the developer's cost.
