Questions, answered.

Quick answers on buying, selling, renting and investing in Dubai. Can't see yours? Ask an Xpert.

Buying

Can non-UAE residents buy property in Dubai?+

Yes. Non-residents can buy property in Dubai's designated freehold areas without restriction.

Are there any restrictions on foreign ownership in Dubai?+

Foreign nationals can buy only in designated freehold areas.

What is a freehold property in Dubai?+

Freehold means you own the property outright. You are free to live in it, lease it or sell it.

What is the difference between freehold and leasehold property?+

Freehold gives you full ownership of the property and the land it stands on, with no time limit. Leasehold gives you the right to use the property for a set period, usually up to 99 years, but you do not own the land.

What are the total costs of buying a property beyond the purchase price?+

Budget for the Dubai Land Department transfer fee of 4% of the property value, a registration trustee fee (for example AED 4,000 on properties over AED 500,000) and agency commission, typically 2%. You may also pay a developer NOC fee of roughly AED 500 to AED 5,000, plus 0.25% of the loan amount to register a mortgage.

Can foreigners get a mortgage in Dubai?+

Yes. Non-resident foreigners can apply to local and international banks in the UAE, subject to eligibility checks. Down payments for non-residents are generally higher than for residents.

What down payment do I need?+

Expats generally need a minimum down payment of 20% on properties under AED 5 million.

Investing & visas

What are the benefits of investing in Dubai real estate?+

You keep your rental income tax-free, returns are strong by global standards, and you invest in a city with modern infrastructure and a fast-growing economy.

Does buying property in Dubai grant a residency visa?+

It can. The visa type and length depend on how much you invest: an Investor Visa (2 years) from AED 750,000, a Green Visa (5 years) from AED 2 million with holding conditions, and a Golden Visa (10 years) from AED 2 million.

Are there property taxes or taxes on rental income in Dubai?+

No. Dubai has no annual property tax on residential homes, no income tax on rental income and no capital gains tax when you sell.

What is the typical return on investment in Dubai?+

Average rental yields typically range from 5% to 9% a year, depending on location and property type. Apartments often yield more than villas.

Which areas are best for investment?+

Popular choices include Downtown Dubai, Dubai Marina, Business Bay, Jumeirah Village Circle (JVC) and Dubai South. Prime tourist areas suit short-term rentals, while emerging communities suit investors looking for long-term growth.

Off-plan

Is it better to invest in off-plan or ready properties?+

Off-plan usually means a lower entry price and flexible payment plans during construction, with the chance of capital growth before completion. Ready property earns rent straight away and has a clearer market value, but needs more money upfront.

How safe is investing in off-plan property?+

Off-plan sales are regulated by the Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA), and developers must hold buyer payments in a registered escrow account. This protects you if a project is delayed or cancelled, but always check the developer's track record and RERA registration.

Is Palm Jebel Ali developed yet, and when will it be completed?+

Palm Jebel Ali is under active development after its relaunch, and no one lives there yet. The first villa handovers are expected between late 2027 and Q4 2028, while the full masterplan will take around two decades.

How does Palm Jebel Ali compare with Palm Jumeirah?+

Palm Jebel Ali is planned to be about 50% larger than Palm Jumeirah. Its masterplan focuses on sustainability and adds more than 110km of new coastline, with villas, apartments and over 80 hotels and resorts.

What is the investment potential of off-plan property on Palm Jebel Ali?+

Its iconic status and relaunch make it a strong candidate for capital growth, and some forecasts expect significant price rises before completion. That makes it attractive to early buyers, though no forecast is guaranteed.

Renting & running costs

How does renting a property in Dubai work?+

You choose a property, sign the tenancy contract, pay the security deposit and agency fee, then register the tenancy with Ejari.

What are the ongoing costs of owning a property?+

Owners pay annual service charges for building upkeep and community amenities, typically AED 12 to AED 30 per square foot depending on the development. You also pay your DEWA utility bills.

Communities

How does life in an established community differ from an upcoming one?+

Established communities have mature landscaping, schools, malls and clinics already in place, and homes you can move into or rent out now. Upcoming communities offer newer designs, more sustainability and smart-home features, flexible off-plan payment plans and more room for growth, but amenities may be limited at first.

Which communities suit families, and which suit luxury, resort-style living?+

For families, Arabian Ranches and The Valley stand out for their parks, schools and community centres. For resort-style living, look at Palm Jumeirah, the waterfront apartments of Dubai Creek Harbour, and the upcoming Palm Jebel Ali and The Oasis.

What are the main differences between Arabian Ranches 1, 2 and 3?+

Arabian Ranches 1 has mature villas on larger plots with established landscaping. Arabian Ranches 2 offers more modern architecture and mid-sized layouts, while Arabian Ranches 3 is the newest phase, with contemporary designs, smart-home technology, a central park and sports facilities.

Are properties in Arabian Ranches freehold?+

Yes. Arabian Ranches is a freehold community, so both UAE nationals and expatriates can own property there outright.

Will the Dubai Creek Tower be built, and how does it affect property values?+

The tower's status and timeline have changed over the years. Dubai Creek Harbour still draws buyers for its waterfront living, Burj Khalifa views and nearby wildlife sanctuary, so values follow the wider community rather than the tower alone.

What types of property are available in Dubai Creek Harbour?+

Mostly high-rise apartments and penthouses with one to four bedrooms and waterfront views. A small number of luxury villas are available in sub-communities such as Creek Beach.

What makes The Valley different?+

The Valley is a self-contained, family-focused community on the Dubai-Al Ain Road, built around nature and outdoor living. It has large green spaces, sports facilities, town centres and themed sub-communities, away from the city but still well connected.

How does The Oasis differ from communities like Dubai Hills Estate?+

The Oasis by Emaar is an ultra-luxury, low-density development with water features and green space covering 25% of the land. It offers only large villas, mansions and super-mansions, whereas Dubai Hills Estate mixes apartments and villas.

What is the idea behind Grand Polo Club & Resort?+

Grand Polo is a collection of luxury villas built around polo and equestrian life. Residents have access to polo fields, stables and riding academies, in a setting that pairs modern luxury with a countryside feel.
